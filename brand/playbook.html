Overview

What this document is, who it serves, and how to use it so that every screen, print piece and sentence feels unmistakably Thamarai.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo set the intent and rules of engagement for the brand system — a shared reference that removes guesswork and keeps a growing team consistent.

This playbook is the governance layer that sits above the website, the app, print collateral and social media. It exists so that a designer in year three makes the same core decisions a designer made on day one — not by memory, but by system.

Why a playbook exists

Healthcare is a trust business. Patients infer competence and safety from consistency: a hospital whose signage, website and forms feel like one confident voice reads as more capable and more caring than one that feels stitched together. Every inconsistency (a stray shade of teal, a second typeface, an off-brand tone) is a small withdrawal from that trust account. This document protects the balance.

How to use it

looks_oneReference before you create. Check the relevant section before starting a new page, asset or campaign.
looks_twoFollow the principle, not just the pixel. When a rule doesn't cover your case, apply §7 Design Principles to decide.
looks_3Propose changes through governance. The brand should evolve — but deliberately. Log proposed changes, version the document, and update tokens in one place.
verified

Best practiceTreat the design tokens (colour, type, spacing) as the source of truth in code. When a value changes here, it should change in one token file and cascade — never be hand-edited page by page.

The brand in one breath

Thamarai means lotus in Tamil — a flower that grows pure and beautiful from difficult water, an ancient symbol of health, renewal and calm. That is the brand's whole idea: advanced, precise medicine delivered with genuine human warmth, for the people of Erode and its surrounding districts.

Brand Promise

The commitment we make to every patient, and the feeling the brand must create to keep it.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo fix the emotional and strategic core of the brand so that colour, type, imagery and words all pull in one direction.

The promise

"Healthcare you can trust."

Expressed as an internal essence: Compassionate Clinical Excellence — the meeting point of a warm human hand and a steady clinical one.

Positioning statement

my_location

For / who / thatFor the people of Erode and surrounding districts, who need serious medical care close to home, Thamarai Multispeciality Hospital is the multispeciality hospital that delivers advanced, technology-driven treatment with genuine compassion — so families feel both safe and cared for.

The three pillars

Every design and content decision should reinforce at least one pillar and never contradict another.

shield_with_heart

Trust

Shows up as: consistency, clarity, credentials, calm. Why: patients are anxious and high-stakes; trust is the currency that lets them choose us.

volunteer_activism

Compassion

Shows up as: warm imagery, reassuring microcopy, generous space, human faces. Why: care is felt, not claimed.

precision_manufacturing

Precision

Shows up as: exact grids, accurate information, restrained design, modern technology cues. Why: precision signals clinical competence.

Brand personality

If Thamarai were a person: a senior consultant who is deeply competent yet unhurried and kind — speaks plainly, never talks down, and makes you feel you are in safe hands. Warm, professional, calm, reassuring.

check_circleThe promise is kept when…
  • An anxious visitor finds the emergency number and "Book Appointment" in seconds.
  • Imagery shows real care and real people, not stock clichés.
  • Copy explains, reassures, and respects the reader's intelligence.
cancelThe promise breaks when…
  • The brand shouts, over-sells, or uses fear to drive action.
  • Design feels cold, clinical-only, or corporate and generic.
  • Jargon or clutter makes a worried patient work harder.

Color Palette

Two connected layers: the identity mark's own colours, and the teal-led digital system drawn from them.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo define exact colour values, the role each plays, and the rules that keep colour meaningful rather than decorative.

psychology

The strategic ideaThe logo's lotus is a blue → teal → green gradient. Blue carries trust, green carries wellness and renewal, and the teal where they meet is healing itself. We pulled the interface's primary colour from that teal, so the product and the mark feel like one organism. Teal also sidesteps the "corporate hospital blue" cliché while still reading calm, clean and clinical.

The 60 / 30 / 10 ruleRoughly 60% neutral (surface + text), 30% teal family, 10% accent (secondary/tertiary). Red is reserved almost entirely for emergency and errors — its scarcity is what makes it read as urgent.

Contrast & accessibility

Every text/background pairing must meet WCAG AA: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and icons. Primary teal on white and white on primary teal both pass. Never place mid-grey text (the outline tone) on light surfaces as body copy — it fails.

check_circleDo
  • Use teal for one clear primary action per view.
  • Keep large fields of calm neutral around content.
  • Reserve red strictly for emergency and error states.
  • Pull every colour from the tokens — never eyeball a hex.
cancelDon't
  • Recolour the logo to match the UI teal.
  • Introduce gradients or shades not in this system.
  • Use red for non-urgent emphasis — it cries wolf.
  • Place two competing primary colours in one view.

Typography

One typeface, a disciplined scale, and rules that keep clinical information effortless to read.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo create a clear, legible hierarchy that works from a 48px headline down to a 12px lab-report caption — on any device.

psychology

Why InterA single family — Inter — carries the whole brand. It was engineered for screens: tall x-height and open shapes make it exceptionally legible at small sizes, which matters when the content is a medicine name or a test result. It is neutral and modern (competence without coldness), variable (one file, many weights), free, and covers the languages our patients read.

Best practices

format_sizeBody never below 16px. Worried readers and older patients must not squint. 16px is the floor for body copy on web.
format_line_spacingLine-height ~1.5 for body. Generous leading reduces cognitive load and improves scannability.
straightenMeasure 60–75 characters. Cap long text blocks so the eye can return to the next line comfortably.
spellcheckTracking only on labels/caps. +0.05em opens up small uppercase labels; body text stays at default tracking.
check_circleDo
  • Use weight and size (not colour) to build hierarchy.
  • Keep one clear H1 per page.
  • Left-align long-form text for readability.
cancelDon't
  • Introduce a second typeface "for headings".
  • Justify body text or centre long paragraphs.
  • Set clinical detail in light-grey low-contrast type.

Logo System

The most valuable asset in the brand. These rules keep it recognisable, legible and respected everywhere it appears.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo govern how the identity mark is used, sized, spaced and protected — and how it must never be altered.

local_florist

MeaningThamarai is Tamil for lotus — purity and healing rising from troubled water. The mark fuses that lotus with a water droplet and a medical cross (life, medicine, care). Its blue → teal → green gradient travels from trust, through healing, to wellness. The navy wordmark grounds it in seriousness; the green tagline keeps it human.

Logo variants

Primary lockup

Mark + wordmark + tagline. Default for headers, footers, print.

The mark

Lotus only. For favicons, app icons and tight spaces.

Clear space & minimum size

crop_freeClear space = the height of the lotus mark on all four sides. Keep this padding free of text, images and edges.
straightenMinimum size — digital: full lockup ≥ 120px wide; the mark alone ≥ 24px. Print: lockup ≥ 22mm wide.
contrastBackgrounds: use on light/neutral surfaces (the mark is transparent). On photography or dark panels, place it inside a white rounded container — never let it fight a busy background.
check_circleDo
  • Use the supplied master files (transparent PNG / vector).
  • Give it room to breathe with full clear space.
  • Use the mark alone when space is tight.
cancelDon't
  • Stretch, squash, rotate or add shadows/outlines.
  • Recolour it, or place it on a clashing colour.
  • Rebuild the wordmark in a different font.
  • Crowd it against headlines, photos or page edges.

Grid & Spacing

The invisible structure that gives every layout calm, order and clinical precision.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo define the spatial system — units, scale, container and rhythm — so layouts feel intentional and are effortless to build consistently.

grid_4x4

Why an 8-point gridAll spacing derives from an 8px base unit. Eight divides cleanly, scales across screen densities, and gives designers and developers a shared vocabulary — "two units", not "about 15-ish pixels". The result is visual rhythm that reads as precision, reinforcing the clinical pillar.

The spacing scale

Layout tokens

Base unit8px — all spacing is a multiple of this

Column gutter24px
Page margin — mobile20px
Page margin — desktop64px
Corner radius8px on cards, inputs and buttons (soft, approachable, not sharp or fully round)
ElevationSoft teal-tinted shadow — 0 4px 20px rgba(13,148,136,.06)
vertical_align_center

Best practicePrefer generous whitespace over dense layouts. Space is not empty — it is calm. It gives anxious readers room to focus and signals confidence. When in doubt, add a unit.

check_circleDo
  • Snap every margin and padding to the 8px scale.
  • Keep consistent gutters and section rhythm.
  • Respect the 1280px container on large screens.
cancelDon't
  • Use arbitrary values like 13px or 27px.
  • Let content run edge-to-edge on wide monitors.
  • Mix sharp and round corners in one interface.

Design Principles

The reasoning that resolves the decisions no rule can fully cover. When guidelines run out, these are the tie-breakers.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo encode the why behind the system so the team makes brand-true decisions in new, un-templated situations.

spa

1 · Clinical calm

Restraint, whitespace and a quiet palette. A calm interface lowers anxiety and reads as competent and in-control.

visibility

2 · Clarity over cleverness

The obvious solution beats the clever one. A worried patient should never have to decode the interface.

favorite

3 · Compassion in the details

Warm photography, reassuring microcopy, human faces. Small kindnesses are where "care" is actually felt.

accessibility_new

4 · Accessible by default

AA contrast, keyboard support, reduced-motion, real alt text — designed in from the start, not bolted on. Care means care for everyone.

repeat

5 · Consistency builds trust

Same components, same patterns, same voice across every touchpoint. Predictability is reassuring in healthcare.

smartphone

6 · Mobile-first

Most patients arrive on a phone, often in a hurry. Design the smallest screen first; the emergency number and booking are always within reach.

emergency

The tie-breakerWhen two good options compete, choose the one that reduces a patient's anxiety fastest. That single test resolves most design debates on this brand.

Buttons

SecondaryOutlined blue — an alternative action of lower priority.

Inputs & forms

labelLabels above fields, always visible — never placeholder-only (placeholders vanish on typing and fail accessibility).
center_focus_strongFocus is obvious — teal border + 3px teal ring, so keyboard users always know where they are.
errorErrors are inline, next to the field, in plain language — not a lump at the top of the form.

Chips, badges & nav

AllSurgicalMedicalDiagnostic NABH Inspired

Filter chips use the pill shape with a clear active state (solid teal). Badges label and categorise. Navigation is consistent across every page, with a persistent "Book Appointment" and the emergency number always reachable.

check_circleDo
  • Reuse components; extend the system, don't fork it.
  • Keep one clear primary action per view.
  • Design every interactive state (hover, focus, disabled).
  • Guarantee 44×44px touch targets.
cancelDon't
  • Invent a one-off button or card style per page.
  • Stack multiple solid-teal buttons competing for attention.
  • Remove focus rings "for looks".
  • Use red buttons for anything non-urgent.

Voice & Tone

How Thamarai sounds. The words carry as much of the promise as the pixels do.

flag

Purpose of this sectionTo keep every sentence — from a hero headline to a form error — sounding like the same calm, competent, caring voice.

Voice attributes

favorite

Warm

Human and kind. We speak to a person, not a "user" or a "case".

lightbulb

Clear

Plain language. Jargon is avoided or explained; sentences are short.

verified_user

Trustworthy

Honest and accurate. We never over-promise or use fear to persuade.

location_city

Locally rooted

Of Erode, for Erode. Familiar, respectful, culturally aware (Tamil & English).

Tone shifts by context

EmergencyDirect, calm, action-first. "Call 1066 now." No marketing language.
MarketingWarm and hopeful, benefit-led, never boastful.
Forms & UISimple, reassuring, guiding. "We'll call to confirm your slot."
Clinical infoPrecise and plain. Accurate first; friendly second.

Terminology

checkSay "care team", not "staff" · "patient", not "customer" · "book an appointment", not "buy"/"purchase".
checkAddress the reader as "you". Prefer active voice and everyday words.
check_circleDo
  • Lead with the patient's need and reassurance.
  • Keep sentences short; explain any medical term.
  • Be honest about what we can and can't promise.
cancelDon't
  • Use fear, urgency-spam or exclamation overload.
  • Hide behind corporate jargon ("revert", "kindly do the needful").
  • Make clinical claims we can't stand behind.
