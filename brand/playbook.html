01

Overview

What this book is, who it serves, and how to use it — so every touchpoint feels unmistakably Thamarai.

Purpose

To set the intent and rules of engagement for the brand — a shared reference that removes guesswork and keeps a growing team consistent as the product evolves.

This book is the governance layer above the website, the app, print collateral and social media. It exists so that a designer in year three makes the same core decisions a designer made on day one — not from memory, but from system.

Why a brand book exists

Healthcare is a trust business. Patients infer competence and safety from consistency: a hospital whose signage, website and forms feel like one confident voice reads as more capable and more caring than one stitched together. Every inconsistency — a stray shade of teal, a second typeface, an off-brand tone — is a small withdrawal from that trust account. This document protects the balance.

Who it is for

A

Designers

Colour, type, spacing, components and the principles that resolve the cases the rules don't cover.

B

Developers

Exact tokens, component specs and accessibility gates that turn intent into shippable code.

C

Marketing & Content

Voice, tone, terminology and application rules for campaigns, social and print.

D

Partners & Vendors

Agencies and printers who need the assets and the guardrails to represent the brand correctly.

How to use it

Reference firstCheck the relevant section before starting any new page, asset or campaign.
Principle over pixelWhen a rule doesn't cover your case, apply §07 Design Principles to decide.
Change by governanceThe brand should evolve — deliberately. Log changes, version the document, update tokens in one place.
Best practice

Treat the design tokens (colour, type, spacing) as the source of truth in code. When a value changes here, it changes in one token file and cascades — never hand-edited page by page.

“Thamarai” means lotus — a flower that grows pure from difficult water. That is the whole idea of the brand.

Advanced, precise medicine delivered with genuine human warmth, for the people of Erode and its surrounding districts.

02

Brand Promise

The commitment we make to every patient — and the feeling the brand must create to keep it.

Purpose

To fix the emotional and strategic core of the brand, so colour, type, imagery and words all pull in one direction.

“Healthcare you can trust.”

Expressed internally as an essence — Compassionate Clinical Excellence — the meeting point of a warm human hand and a steady clinical one.

Positioning

For / who / that

For the people of Erode and surrounding districts who need serious medical care close to home, Thamarai Multispeciality Hospital is the multispeciality hospital that delivers advanced, technology-driven treatment with genuine compassion — so families feel both safe and cared for.

The three pillars

Every decision should reinforce at least one pillar and contradict none.

01

Trust

Shows up as consistency, clarity, credentials and calm. Trust is the currency that lets an anxious patient choose us.

02

Compassion

Shows up as warm imagery, reassuring microcopy, generous space and human faces. Care is felt, not claimed.

03

Precision

Shows up as exact grids, accurate information, restraint and modern technology cues. Precision signals competence.

Personality

If Thamarai were a person: a senior consultant who is deeply competent yet unhurried and kind — speaks plainly, never talks down, and makes you feel you are in safe hands.

The promise is kept when
  • An anxious visitor finds the emergency number and “Book Appointment” in seconds.
  • Imagery shows real care and real people, not stock clichés.
  • Copy explains, reassures and respects the reader.
The promise breaks when
  • The brand shouts, over-sells, or uses fear to drive action.
  • Design feels cold, clinical-only, or generic and corporate.
  • Jargon or clutter makes a worried patient work harder.
03

Colour Palette

Two connected layers: the identity mark's own colours, and the teal-led digital system drawn from them.

Purpose

To define exact colour values, the role each plays, and the rules that keep colour meaningful rather than decorative.

The strategic idea

The logo's lotus is a blue → teal → green gradient. Blue carries trust, green carries wellness and renewal, and the teal where they meet is healing itself. We pulled the interface's primary colour from that teal, so product and mark feel like one organism — and sidestepped the “corporate hospital blue” cliché while staying calm, clean and clinical.

The 60 / 30 / 10 rule

Roughly 60% neutral, 30% teal family, 10% accent. Red is reserved almost entirely for emergency and errors — its scarcity is what makes it read as urgent.

Do
  • Use teal for one clear primary action per view.
  • Keep large fields of calm neutral around content.
  • Pull every colour from the tokens — never eyeball a hex.
Don't
  • Recolour the logo to match the UI teal.
  • Add gradients or shades not in this system.
  • Use red for non-urgent emphasis — it cries wolf.
04

Typography

One typeface, a disciplined scale, and rules that keep clinical information effortless to read.

Purpose

To create a clear, legible hierarchy that works from a 48px headline down to a 12px lab-report caption, on any device.

Why Inter

A single family — Inter — carries the whole brand. Engineered for screens, its tall x-height and open shapes make it exceptionally legible at small sizes, which matters when the content is a medicine name or a test result. Neutral, modern, variable, free, and broad in language coverage.

Best practices

Body floorNever below 16px on web — worried and older readers must not squint.
Line-height~1.5 for body; generous leading lowers cognitive load.
Measure60–75 characters per line so the eye returns comfortably.
TrackingOnly on labels & caps (+0.05em); body stays at default.
HierarchyBuild with size & weight, not colour. One H1 per page.
Do
  • Use weight and size to build hierarchy.
  • Left-align long-form text.
Don't
  • Add a second typeface “for headings”.
  • Justify body or set clinical detail in light grey.
05

Logo System

The most valuable asset in the brand. These rules keep it recognisable, legible and respected everywhere it appears.

Purpose

To govern how the identity mark is used, sized, spaced and protected — and how it must never be altered.

Meaning

Thamarai is Tamil for lotus — purity and healing rising from troubled water. The mark fuses that lotus with a water droplet and a medical cross (life, medicine, care). Its blue → teal → green gradient travels from trust, through healing, to wellness. The navy wordmark grounds it in seriousness; the green tagline keeps it human.

Variants

Primary lockup

Mark + wordmark + tagline. Default for headers, footers and print.

The mark

Lotus only. For favicons, app icons and tight spaces.

Clear space, size & backgrounds

Clear spaceKeep padding equal to the height of the lotus mark on all four sides, free of text, images and edges.
Minimum — digitalFull lockup ≥ 120px wide; the mark alone ≥ 24px.
Minimum — printLockup ≥ 22mm wide.
BackgroundsUse on light / neutral surfaces (the mark is transparent). On photography or dark panels, place it inside a white rounded container.
Do
  • Use the supplied master files (transparent PNG / vector).
  • Give it full clear space to breathe.
  • Use the mark alone when space is tight.
Don't
  • Stretch, squash, rotate or add shadows.
  • Recolour it or rebuild the wordmark in another font.
  • Crowd it against headlines, photos or edges.
06

Grid & Spacing

The invisible structure that gives every layout calm, order and clinical precision.

Purpose

To define the spatial system — units, scale, container and rhythm — so layouts feel intentional and are effortless to build consistently.

Why an 8-point grid

All spacing derives from an 8px base unit. Eight divides cleanly, scales across screen densities, and gives designers and developers a shared vocabulary — “two units”, not “about 15-ish pixels”. The rhythm reads as precision, reinforcing the clinical pillar.

Best practice

Prefer generous whitespace over dense layouts. Space is not empty — it is calm. It gives anxious readers room to focus and signals confidence. When in doubt, add a unit.

07

Design Principles

The reasoning that resolves the decisions no rule can fully cover. When guidelines run out, these are the tie-breakers.

Purpose

To encode the why behind the system, so the team makes brand-true decisions in new, un-templated situations.

01

Clinical calm

Restraint, whitespace and a quiet palette. A calm interface lowers anxiety and reads as in-control.

02

Clarity over cleverness

The obvious solution beats the clever one. A worried patient should never decode the interface.

03

Compassion in the details

Warm photography, reassuring microcopy, human faces. Small kindnesses are where care is felt.

04

Accessible by default

AA contrast, keyboard support, reduced-motion, real alt text — designed in, not bolted on.

05

Consistency builds trust

Same components, patterns and voice everywhere. Predictability is reassuring in healthcare.

06

Mobile-first

Most patients arrive on a phone, often in a hurry. Design the smallest screen first.

The tie-breaker

When two good options compete, choose the one that reduces a patient's anxiety fastest. That single test resolves most design debates on this brand.

Do
  • Reuse components; extend the system, don't fork it.

Don't

  • Stack competing solid-teal buttons.
  • Remove focus rings “for looks”.

10

Voice & Tone

How Thamarai sounds. The words carry as much of the promise as the pixels do.

Purpose

To keep every sentence — from a hero headline to a form error — sounding like the same calm, competent, caring voice.

Voice attributes

01

Warm

Human and kind. We speak to a person, not a “user” or a “case”.

02

Clear

Plain language. Jargon is avoided or explained; sentences are short.

03

Trustworthy

Honest and accurate. We never over-promise or use fear to persuade.

04

Locally rooted

Of Erode, for Erode. Respectful and culturally aware (Tamil & English).

Tone by context

EmergencyDirect, calm, action-first. “Call 1066 now.” No marketing language.
MarketingWarm and hopeful, benefit-led, never boastful.
Forms & UISimple, reassuring, guiding. “We'll call to confirm your slot.”
ClinicalPrecise and plain. Accurate first; friendly second.

Before & after

Off-brand
Submit your details and our executives will revert to you at the earliest regarding your query.
On-brand

Off-brand

On-brand

Terminology

Say “care team” not “staff” · “patient” not “customer” · “book an appointment” not “buy”. Address the reader as “you”; prefer active voice and everyday words.
